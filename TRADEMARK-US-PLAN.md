# GET LUCKY — United States trade mark plan (Class 41 first)

**Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd · 3 October 2026 · working plan, not legal advice**

## South African position (official action of 15 September 2026)

CIPC application **2025/33406, GET LUCKY, class 41**, in the name of Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd,
attorneys Viollier IP Attorneys (ref TM1574ZA41BLG). The Registrar will accept subject to one
condition only: **a disclaimer of the word "LUCKY"** apart from the mark. That is routine and
harmless; the mark GET LUCKY as a whole is protected, only the single word LUCKY is not claimed
on its own. Response due within three months, **by 15 December 2026**. After acceptance the mark
is advertised in the Patent Journal, a three-month opposition window runs, and the certificate
issues (allow six to twelve months at current CIPC pace). The registration dates back to the
2025 filing date.

Two consequences for the US:

- The application number is a 2025 serial, so the filing is more than six months old and the
  **Paris Convention priority has lapsed**. The US application gets its own filing date; file soon.
- The SA mark is the two words **GET LUCKY**, not GET LUCKY GOLF CLUB. A US application can rely
  on the SA registration under Section 44(e) only for the **same mark**. So: file GET LUCKY in the
  US on intent-to-use plus 44(e) if the clearance search allows; if the US search shows the two
  words are blocked by casino marks, file GET LUCKY GOLF CLUB on intent-to-use alone.

Context: South Africa is not in the Madrid system, so the US is a direct national filing at the
USPTO. Foreign-domiciled applicants must be represented by a US-licensed attorney for every filing.

## Quality rules that save money

- Services wording from the ID Manual, nothing custom: avoids $200 per class in surcharges and the most common office action.
- One applicant name, matching the SA record and the company registration; mismatches cost an amendment.
- A US address is not needed, but a domicile address for the company and an attorney of record are.
- Keep every specimen dated and in its original form from day one of the pilot; recreated specimens get refused.
- Do not file the logo until the words have cleared examination; the logo inherits the same refusals at twice the cost.

**The South African company owns the mark.** Get Lucky Golf Club (Pty) Ltd is the applicant in the US, in the same name as the CIPC record, for four reasons:

1. Section 44(e) only works when the US applicant is the owner of the home registration. The SA company is that owner.
2. The Santam, Ernie Els and RMB agreements sit with the SA company. The brand should sit with the contracts.
3. A single owner makes the later UK, EU, Korea and China filings, and any Madrid International Registration, one portfolio with one renewal calendar.
4. An intent-to-use US application cannot be assigned before use is shown, except together with the whole business. Changing the owner later is expensive or impossible, so the choice is made now.

The US entity operates under a written licence from the SA company: non-exclusive or exclusive for the US, royalty as the group's transfer-pricing advice requires, and a quality-control clause (the SA company approves how the mark is used). Without quality control the licence is "naked" and can weaken the US registration. Use by the licensee counts as use by the owner for the Statement of Use, so the pilot run by the US entity still supports the SA company's application. Record the licence only if the US attorney advises it; the USPTO does not require it.
