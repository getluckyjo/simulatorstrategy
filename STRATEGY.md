jackpot layer inside the world's simulator platforms — a $1 swing at a real, underwritten prize,

contact with three things the August document did not have to face: the real simulator ace rate,
the competitors who already sell an insured screen ace in Korea, and the fact that nobody at
Golfzon has heard of us yet.

## 3. The market, and why now

**Size.** Global simulator hardware and software is a $2.1–2.4bn market today, roughly doubling
by the mid-2030s. North America is ~41% of it. That is the equipment market; the *play* market
sits on top of it and is where we live.

**Korea is the proof that people will pay to hit shots indoors at scale.** A $1.6bn screen-golf
industry, 8,700+ venues, ~94 million simulator rounds a year — nearly twice the rounds played on
grass. Golfzon holds ~60%, Kakao VX ~25% (2,100+ Friends Screen lounges), the rest independents.

**America is where it is happening next.** 48.1M Americans touched golf in 2025; 19M of them only
off-course. Simulator users went from 3.8M (2015) to 8M+ (2024). Commercial simulator businesses
passed 1,500 by late 2025 — nearly tripled since 2022 — most with 3–4 bays and three-quarters
selling memberships. Golfzon has 120+ US commercial facilities, X-Golf 130+, Five Iron 50+ venues
with 500+ Trackman bays, Topgolf 103 venues of ~100 bays each.

## 5. Which market first — America, and here is why

Korea is not saturated for a jackpot; it is saturated for the insurance-reimbursement form, priced
to the bone by five carriers, on a platform (Golfzon) that already has an embedded insurer, in a
legal regime where the prize-contest form hits the Criminal Act and the Speculative Acts law. We can
win there — as an insurance-wrapped product with a local carrier and Santam Re behind it — but it
is a phase-two fight and we should not lead with it.

The US is the better first bet because:

1. **The demand is there and untapped.** 19M off-course-only golfers, 8M+ simulator users,
   1,500+ commercial venues tripling in three years — and no insured ace product for consumers.
2. **The precedents are live.** Golfzon Tour, Five Iron Tournaments, NEXT and Skill Strike all run
   paid-entry real-money play with state carve-outs and facility-level responsibility. We are not
   asking a US operator to do something new; we are asking them to add a better prize.
3. **The insurance market is mature.** Prize indemnity is a regulated line (Odds On is underwritten
   by Everest, A+; Lloyd's coverholders write it). Santam can lead, co-insure or reinsure, and
   Indwe can place it.
4. **Every operator's decision-maker is in the US.** Golfzon's Global Business Division is in
   Virginia. Versant, Five Iron, Topgolf and Full Swing are American. Trackman's US sales
   leadership sits in the US.
5. **Ernie's equity is strongest there.** Two U.S. Opens, a Florida home, a Golf Channel regular —
   and Golfzon is the official simulator of the U.S. Open.
6. **One deal in the US is worth more than one deal in Korea** to the company's valuation story:
   it proves the product travels, and it is the market every other operator benchmarks.

**So: Golfzon stays the lead target, pitched in the US through Golfzon America; Five Iron is the
fast pilot; Versant/Full Swing and Trackman run in parallel. Korea is phase two, entered with
Golfzon HQ's blessing and a Korean carrier.** The current model's volume base (94M Korean rounds)
has to be rebuilt on a US base — flagged for the investor site, not done here.

## 6. Product and pricing that actually underwrites

### 6a. The ace rate

Golfzon's own network data, mined by GOLF.com, gives us the number the August document never had.
In 2022 Golfzon users recorded 7.63 billion shots, roughly 1 billion of them tee shots on par-3s,
and **143,020 holes-in-one** in completed 18-hole rounds — **one ace every 6,992 par-3 swings**.
That is 392 aces a day, which squares with the "300+ a day" a Korean screen-golf company reported
separately. GOLF.com's framing: a simulator ace is about 28% more likely than one outdoors (the
National Hole-in-One Registry puts the average player at 12,000:1 per shot, a low handicapper at
5,000:1, a Tour player at 3,000:1). For colour: a content creator needed 1,736 swings at Pebble's
7th for an ace; a streamer needed 2,627.

Two cautions. First, 1 in 7,000 is a network average across every hole and every player. Korea's
"명당" — short, funnel-green par-3s that produce most of the aces — run far hotter, and a golfer who
chooses the hole chooses the odds. Second, small-ticket ace policies like CREW ($0.86 a round for up
to $150) are priced for expense loading on a tiny premium, not for frequency, so they don't tell us
the rate. Golfzon's data does.

**Working assumption:** base **1 in 7,000** per qualifying attempt (Golfzon 2022); stress
**1 in 2,500** (a player-selected hole set); course 1 in 12,500. Santam's measured number on our
whitelist replaces it.

### 6b. What that does to $1 → $1,000 at 24%

Premium the insurer needs at a 60% target loss ratio = prize ÷ attempts-per-ace ÷ 0.6 ÷ entry:

| Ace rate | $1 → $250 | $1 → $500 | $1 → $1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 in 12,500 (course) | 3% | 7% | 13% |
| **1 in 6,992 (Golfzon 2022 — base)** | 6% | **12%** | **24%** |
| 1 in 3,434 (conservative reading of "300 a day") | 12% | 24% | 49% |
| 1 in 2,500 (stress — selected holes) | 17% | 33% | 67% |
| 1 in 1,000 (extreme) | 42% | 83% | 167% |

The August product ($1 → $1,000 at 24%) prices at exactly the network average — with no margin for
hole selection, none for a hot month, and none for the fact that a jackpot attracts the golfers
most likely to hit it. **A 500× ladder holds the 24% cession with a 2× safety margin at the
measured rate.** 250× is the fallback if a whitelist can't hold the rate.

### 6d. The Million Dollar Shot — $1,000 → $1,000,000 on the slip

A 1,000× multiple prices exactly like $1 → $1,000: at 1 in 7,000 the insurer's expected claim is
$143 a swing and a 24% cession ($240) gives a 60% loss ratio with no margin. The difference is who
buys it. Nobody paying $1,000 a swing is an average golfer, and a grinder who finds a soft hole has
positive expected value:

| Who buys a $1,000 swing | Ace rate | Premium at 60% LR | % of entry |
|---|---|---|---|
| Network average | 1 in 7,000 | $238 | 24% |
| Low handicapper | 1 in 5,000 | $333 | 33% |
| Tour-level player | 1 in 3,000 | $556 | 56% |
| A "명당" hole with soft settings | 1 in 2,000 | $833 | 83% |

**Rules that make it bindable:** (1) **one flat $1,000,000 prize for everyone; professionals
excluded** — PGA of America members, anyone holding or having held tour status (PGA Tour, DP World,
LPGA, Korn Ferry, Champions, Sunshine, Asian, Korean tours) and anyone who has earned prize money
as a professional; declared at purchase against ID, forfeit if false. No handicap tiers — the
prize is the same for a 20-handicap and a scratch amateur. The handicap is still recorded, for the
insurer's rate card, not for eligibility. With pros out, the realised rate should sit between the
network average (1 in 7,000) and the low-handicap rate (1 in 5,000), so plan on a **~45% cession**;
(2) the Get Lucky hole for this tier is a designated 170-yard-plus par-3 per course, built into the software, standard settings locked; (3) one attempt per player
per round, three a day; (4) KYC at purchase, 1099 on the win, paid entry in Green states only —
never free-to-play, which would trip sweepstakes registration and bonding at $1M; (5) an aggregate
cap and a layer above Santam's $100,000 largest-single-event limit (Santam Re, Lloyd's or Everest).

**What it earns.** At a 45% cession: Get Lucky $250 a swing, operator $300, insurer $450
against ~$200 expected claims. If 0.1% of the 2029 model's 1.41M entrants take one shot a year
(1,410 swings): $353k to Get Lucky, $423k to the operator, ~25% chance of a $1M winner in the year.
If Santam prices at 1 in 7,000 and holds 24%, the split reverts to 46/30/24 and the tier pays us
$460 a swing — that is the upside case, not the plan. Because we designate the hole and the software records every attempt on it, the tier's realised ace rate is measured from day one — Santam prices the $1M tier on our own telemetry within a quarter.

**Eyes open.** Demand is unproven — Skill Strike caps at $20, Five Iron at $10, NEXT at $130 a
tournament — so it launches as a flagship-venue test (Golfzon Social, CityGolf Nashville, the PGA
Show floor) branded *The Million Dollar Shot — verified players only*, and "$1,000 a swing" in a
family venue is a conversation to have with Golfzon before it is in a deck. The $1 slip stays the
door; the $1,000 tier is the story and the barrier — a continuously bound seven-figure per-shot
programme with handicap-adjusted pricing is not something a promoter can copy with an event policy.

Rule of thumb for any conversation: **supportable prize ≈ entry × premium% × 0.6 × attempts-per-ace.**

**Product rules that are really pricing tools:**
- **The Get Lucky hole.** One designated par-3 per course, chosen by us and built into the operator's
  software — the player never picks the hole. That removes the 명당 adverse-selection problem at the
  root, gives Santam a measured ace rate per designated hole (a real per-hole rate card, not a
  whitelist), and lets us designate a longer hole for higher tiers.
- One insured entry per hole per round; standard difficulty, green speed and wind settings locked
  for insured shots; no mulligans, no practice-mode entries.
- Verification is the simulator's own telemetry (ball in hole, launch data, session ID, member ID)
  plus the bay camera where it exists — the "machine truth" implementation already in §24 of the
  app spec. No claim is paid on a screenshot.
- Insurer → winner, directly. Get Lucky is never in the funds flow, exactly as on course.

**Santam deliverables before any operator sees a number:**
1. Written indication at simulator frequency for a 500× and a 1,000× ladder, per-hole rate card — and a separate indication for the flat $1,000 → $1M tier (professionals excluded), with the layer above the $100,000 single-event limit.
2. Aggregate cap and largest-single-event limit for a pilot (assume 100 venues × 90 days).
3. Territory: South Africa only, or global via Santam Re / fronting / co-insurance with a US
   carrier (Everest via Odds On, or a Lloyd's coverholder) and, later, a Korean carrier.
4. Claims protocol for telemetry-based verification and a fraud clause (Korea has seen staged aces).
