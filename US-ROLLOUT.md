## 1. The market in numbers

| | Figure | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Americans who touched golf in 2025 | **48.1M** — 29.1M on-course, **19M off-course only** (ranges, simulators, Topgolf) | NGF 2026 |
| Simulator / screen-golf users | **8.1M in 2024**, up from 3.6M five years earlier (+126%) | NGF white paper 2025 |
| Who they are | **51% are "non-golfers"** (no on-course round in 12 months), up from 42% a decade ago; three-quarters say they will return; groups of ~3 per bay; younger and more diverse than the on-course base | NGF |
| Commercial simulator businesses | **1,500+** by late 2025, nearly tripled since 2022; median 4 bays, mean 5.9 | NGF / Tabski |

| North America's share of the global simulator market | **46.6%** of a $2.1bn 2025 market | Grand View, Jun 2026 |
| Golfers by state | **California ~2.8M**; Texas, Florida, New York, Pennsylvania, Illinois and Ohio each **>1M**; Florida has the most courses (1,290) and the most rounds | NGF state reports |

**What a bay actually does.** A staffed venue sells ~**1,900 bay-hours per bay a year** (36% blended
utilisation), at a **$55 session** of ~90 minutes with **3 players** and **$40 of food and drink** —
roughly **$100 per visit** and **$95k–$115k of revenue per bay a year** (X-Golf's FY2025 franchise
disclosure across 114 outlets: median $94,970 per simulator). Memberships run $99–$299 a month,
leagues $199 a season, corporate buyouts $2,500–$6,000.

**Seasonality is the shape of this business.** Peak season is **November to April at ~50%
utilisation** (December 51%); summer runs **~23%**, September is the trough at 21%. The best
month earns 2.6× the worst. Operators' single biggest problem is filling weekday daytime — 57% of
weekday bay-hours are 9am–4pm and mostly empty. That is a product opening for us: a 60-second
Million Dollar Shot is exactly what a venue wants to sell at 2pm on a Tuesday.

## 3. Reading the map

1. **Six of the seven biggest golf states are Green** — California, Texas, Florida, New York,
   Illinois, Ohio. Only Pennsylvania (🟡) is not. The paid product reaches most of the golfers in
   America without a single Amber opinion.
2. **The cold-weather engine is half Green, half Amber.** Illinois, Ohio, Wisconsin, Massachusetts
   and New York are Green; Michigan, Minnesota, Missouri, Pennsylvania are Amber. Two opinions —
   Michigan and Pennsylvania — unlock 30+ X-Golf and Five Iron venues.
3. **New York and Chicago are where every operator overlaps.** Five Iron (11 venues), Golfzon
   Social (3), Golfzon Range (2), X-Golf (22 in IL/NY), and both are Green. That is the pilot.
4. **Florida is ours in a way it is not Five Iron's.** AGO 90-58 blesses a fixed, sponsor-funded
   prize; pooled-entry products have to stay out. Florida has the most courses, the most rounds,
   Ernie's home, the PGA Show, and Topgolf's second-largest cluster.
5. **The Red states are marketing states, not volume states** — with one exception. Nashville
   (CityGolf, Five Iron, X-Golf ×4, Topgolf) is a real market and it is Red; it gets the
   free-play structure and a sponsor.
6. **Golfzon's own growth markets are awkward:** Arizona is Red, Nevada is Amber. Texas is Green.
   Lead the Golfzon conversation with Texas, New York and Illinois volume, not Phoenix.

## 4. The customer, and the two products in the bay

| | The $1 slip | The Million Dollar Shot |
|---|---|---|
| Who | The group of three at 7pm; 51% of them don't play on grass. They spend ~$100 a visit; $1 is invisible | The corporate host, the birthday group, the member who just birdied, the Vegas bay. The **event buyer** is the natural $1,000 payer — a $2,500–$6,000 buyout with one Million Dollar Shot for the guest of honour |
| When | Every visit — the designated hole appears in the round; a standalone **60-second "Get Lucky hole" mode** in the bay menu for walk-ups and daytime | Flagship venues, leagues finals, event packages, the PGA Show floor, a monthly live "Million Dollar Shot" segment |
| Why they buy | The moment. Every ace becomes a video (Golfzon already auto-captures it) — the content loop is free | The story. Nobody else in the building has a swing at a million |
| Attach assumption | **5% / 10% / 20%** of player-sessions (conservative / base / strong). For reference, Five Iron's beta had 1,000 players log 20,000 entries with 65% returning in the month | **0.05% / 0.10%** of ladder entrants take one shot a year |

Selling motions inside the venue: the slip at booking (prepaid bays cancel less — operators like
prepaid add-ons), at the bay screen, and through leagues — Five Iron's leagues already run weekly
closest-to-the-pin prizes; we are the CTP prize upgrade. Daytime: the Get Lucky hole as the
venue's 2pm product.

## 5. The volume model — a US base, not a Korean one

The investor model's simulator line is Korea-only. This is the US base that should replace it
(flagged for `investgetlucky`, not changed here).

**Inputs:** 1,900 bay-hours sold per bay a year; ~1.5 hours a session; 3 players a bay →
**~3,800 player-sessions per bay a year**; one designated-hole opportunity per player-session;
Get Lucky $0.46 per $1 entry; Tier C $250 per swing at a 45% cession; free-play (Structure B)
licence assumed at **$40 per bay per month**.

| | Pilot — Jan–Apr 2027 | National year 1 — Nov 2027–Oct 2028 | Year 2 — Nov 2028–Oct 2029 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paid bays live (Green states) | 160 (Five Iron NYC + Chicago; Golfzon Social) | ~1,500 (Five Iron 300 · X-Golf 390 · Golfzon 300 · independents 500) | ~3,000 |
| Free-play bays (Amber/Red) | — | ~700 | ~1,500 |
| Player-sessions on paid bays | 274k (peak-season share) | 5.7M | 11.4M |
| **Ladder entries at 5% / 10% / 20%** | 14k / 27k / 55k | **285k / 570k / 1.14M** | 570k / 1.14M / 2.28M |
| Get Lucky from the ladder | $6k / $13k / $25k | $131k / **$262k** / $524k | $262k / $524k / $1.05M |
| Tier C swings (0.1% of entrants) | ~30 | 570 | 1,140 |
| Get Lucky from Tier C | $7k | **$143k** | $285k |
| Free-play licences | — | **$336k** | $720k |
| **Get Lucky revenue (base case)** | **~$20k** | **~$740k** | **~$1.5M** |
| Operator revenue (base) | ~$13k | ~$340k | ~$680k |
| Insurer premium written (base) | ~$16k | ~$390k | ~$790k |

For scale: the current investor model carries $346k of simulator revenue in 2028 and $649k in
2029 on a Korea-only base. The US base at a 10% attach roughly doubles both, and it does so on
Green-state venues that exist today.

**The pilot is not a revenue event** — 160 bays for four months is ~$20k. It is the ace rate by
designated hole, the attach rate by time of day, the Tier C demand test, and the telemetry that
prices Santam's rate card. Say that to the operator and the investor before anyone asks.

Two calendar rules: **never launch paid in summer** (the trough hides the attach rate), and
**every big moment sits in the season** — PGA Show in January, national launch in November.

## 9. Risks specific to the US roll-out

| Risk | What we do |
|---|---|
| Attach rate is a guess | The pilot measures it at 160 bays before anyone commits a national number |
| Tier C demand is unproven | Flagship venues and event packages only until the pilot says otherwise |
| Season timing | Paid pilot Jan–Apr; national launch November; nothing paid launches in summer |
| Amber states hold a third of the cold-weather bays | Michigan and Pennsylvania opinions in Phase 2; free-play there meanwhile |
| Skill Strike gets there first on Full Swing | Move on Golfzon and Trackman, where there is no money game; pitch Versant as the prize layer |
| Payment rails and chargebacks on $1,000 swings | Prepaid in-app; KYC; the operator's wallet (Lucra at Five Iron) carries the risk framework already |
| Budget | The R8m round does not fund a US-first launch on its own — say so to the shareholders now and make the operator and the sponsor carry launch marketing |
