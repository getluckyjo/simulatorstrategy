## 3. Ace-rate evidence

| Source | What it says | Per-attempt rate |
|---|---|---|
| **Golfzon network data, 2022** (mined by GOLF.com) | 7.63bn shots; ~1.0bn par-3 tee shots; **143,020 holes-in-one** in completed 18-hole rounds | **1 in 6,992** — the network average across every hole and every player |
| National Hole-in-One Registry | Per-shot odds by ability | Tour player 1 in 3,000 · low handicapper 1 in 5,000 · average golfer 1 in 12,000 |

| Korean insurers (Lotte CREW, NH, Carrot × Golfzon) | Sell screen-golf ace cover at $0.86–$1.20 a round for $112–$150 of costs | Priced for expense loading, not frequency; confirms the price point, not the rate |
| Our on-course book | 10,000 entries at 1 in 12,500 assumed | Grass, not screen |

**Pricing bases we propose:**

| | Tiers A and B | Tier C |
|---|---|---|
| Base | **1 in 7,000** (Golfzon network average) | **1 in 5,000** — with professionals excluded, the buyers of a $1,000 swing will still skew to better amateurs |
| Stress | 1 in 2,500 | 1 in 3,000 |
| Rate review | On our own telemetry after 90 days, by designated hole | Same |

The single biggest driver of the realised rate is **which hole**. Korean data shows specific short,
funnel-green par-3s produce most of the aces. That is why the product designates one Get Lucky
hole per course, chosen by us and built into the operator's software — the player never picks it —
and why Santam gets a measured rate per designated hole rather than a network average.

## 4. Premium and loss ratio by tier

Premium required = prize ÷ attempts-per-ace ÷ target loss ratio. Target loss ratio 60%.

**Tier A — $1 → $500 (500×), cession 24% = $0.24**

| Ace rate | Expected claim | Loss ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 in 12,500 (grass) | $0.04 | 17% |
| **1 in 7,000 (base)** | **$0.071** | **30%** |
| 1 in 5,000 | $0.10 | 42% |
| 1 in 2,500 (stress) | $0.20 | 83% |

**Tier B — $1 → $1,000 (1,000×), cession 24%**

| Ace rate | Expected claim | Loss ratio |
|---|---|---|
| **1 in 7,000 (base)** | **$0.143** | **60%** — no margin |
| 1 in 5,000 | $0.20 | 83% |

**Tier C — $1,000 → $1,000,000, cession 45% = $450**

| Ace rate | Expected claim | Loss ratio |
|---|---|---|
| 1 in 7,000 | $143 | 32% |
| **1 in 5,000 (base)** | **$200** | **44%** |
| 1 in 3,000 (stress) | $333 | 74% |
| 1 in 2,500 | $400 | 89% |

If Santam is comfortable pricing Tier C at the network average, a 24% cession ($240) gives a 60%
loss ratio at 1 in 7,000. We have planned on 45% so that the tier is bindable on day one and the
rate can come down on our data.

## 5. Volume and exposure

Volumes are the current plan's simulator ramp (US-first, one lead operator), and Tier C is
assumed at 0.05–0.10% of ladder entrants taking one shot a year.

| Expected Tier C claims (1 in 5,000) | $28k → $56k | $75k → $150k | **$141k → $282k** |
| P(at least one $1M claim in the year) | 3% → 5% | 7% → 14% | **13% → 25%** |
| P(two or more $1M claims in the year) | <0.2% | 0.3% → 1.0% | **0.9% → 3.3%** |

**Pilot (90 days, up to 100 venues):** assume 20 ladder entries per venue per day → ~180,000
entries, ~$43,000 of ladder premium, ~26 expected aces; Tier C at flagship venues only, assume
≤ 100 attempts → $45,000 premium, ~2% chance of a $1M claim.

**Proposed aggregate structure for Tier C:** annual aggregate of **$2,000,000** in year one
(two winners; the chance of a third is under 0.3% at 2029 volumes), reviewed with volume.

**Optional:** price the $1,000,000 both as a lump sum and as **$50,000 a year for 20 years** —
the common structure for seven-figure ace prizes. The annuity form reduces the present-value
exposure by roughly a third and may let the cession come down; we would market it honestly
either way.

## 7. Fund flow and premium mechanics

1. Golfer pays the entry inside the operator's app (Golfzon / Five Iron wallet).
2. Operator settles Get Lucky's share and the insurer's cession per attempt.
3. Get Lucky remits the cession to Indwe on a **monthly bordereau** by tier, by designated hole,
   with attempts, aces and claims; deposit and minimum premium as Santam requires.
4. A claim is notified with the telemetry pack; the insurer pays the winner directly.
5. Get Lucky is never in the funds flow for prizes.
