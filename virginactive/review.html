The Virgin Golf bay model

One model, two readers. Virgin Active reads it to see what 200 bays earn a chain that puts up no capital. RMB reads it to see the same cash servicing a R162m asset-finance facility. Every number below comes out of virginactive/model.py, so a changed assumption changes the whole page.

The structure

Virgin Golf (Pty) Ltd, a Get Lucky company with Ernie Els as founding partner, buys, installs and operates 200 Golfzon bays inside Virgin Active clubs under a ten-year exclusive concession. RMB finances the equipment against the concession agreement and the bays themselves. Virgin Active provides the room, the power and its members, bills the Virgin Golf membership add-on on the member's account, and takes a fifth of every rand the bays earn.

Virgin Active

Puts in
A 2-bay zone (about 110 m²) in 100 clubs and a 4-bay zone in 12 Collection and flagship clubs, built into planned refurbishments. Power, cleaning, access control. Bay booking in the Virgin Active app. Marketing to 623,000 members. A ten-year exclusive.
Gets out
20% of bay revenue: R36m a year at maturity, about R345k a year for a 2-bay zone and R1,082k for a 4-bay Collection zone, with no capital and no operating risk. A member retention product. Ernie Els on the door. First call on Virgin Golf for the UK, Italy, Australia and Asia.

Virgin Golf

Puts in
R18m of equity as the 10% deposit, plus the ramp shortfall while phases commission: about R10m in total in the base case, R26m in the low case. The Golfzon relationship, the Ernie Els brand, coaching content, leagues, the insured-shot product, operations and a golf host in every Collection club.
Gets out
EBITDA of R108m a year at maturity, R65m after RMB. Payback on a bay from its own EBITDA in under two years. A 200-bay reference customer to take to Golfzon and to the rest of the Virgin Active group.

RMB

Puts in
R162m in three phased drawdowns, each 12 months interest-only while the phase commissions and ramps, then 60 months amortising at prime + 1.5% (12.0% today). Security over the bays and a cession of the concession agreement and the naming sponsorship.
Gets out
Debt cover of 1.9× in 2029 rising to 2.5× at maturity in the base case, 1.6× at maturity in the low case. A national fitness chain as the venue, Santam behind the prize product, and equipment that Golfzon services under contract.

A bay at maturity

Bay hire is the engine. A bay is bookable 15 hours a day, 360 days a year. The base case has a metro Play bay booked five hours a day at a blended R360 an hour across peak, off-peak, member and non-member rates. Virgin Active padel is R400 an hour today; Johannesburg and Cape Town simulator venues charge R200 to R400 a bay. Golfzon's own venue guidance is ten hours a day at $50; the base case is half the hours at a third of the rate.

What Virgin Active sees

  • R36m a year of concession income across 112 clubs at maturity, for space it is refurbishing anyway. That is about R345k a year from a 2-bay zone and R1,082k from a 4-bay Collection zone.
  • 365k booked bay-hours a year, roughly 0.9 million player visits. Every one is a member reason to come to the club on a winter weeknight.
  • 7,696 members on a golf add-on at R299 a month, billed by Virgin Active, with a share to Virgin Active and a reason not to cancel.
  • Zero capital, zero operating risk. RMB carries the equipment, Golfzon services it, Virgin Golf runs it. Virgin Active can take a buy-out option on the bays at the end of the concession if it wants them.

Assumptions and where they come from

  • Golfzon hardware: TwoVision NX $60,000, Vision Standard $35,000, GDR Plus $22,000, from Golfzon's published commercial ranges ($25k to $90k a bay). To be replaced by the Golfzon quote in October.
  • Rates: Virgin Active padel R400 an hour; South African simulator venues R200 to R400 a bay an hour (Par 72, Golf Bar, Golf Sim Centre). Blended yields: Signature R520, Play R360, Practice R240.
  • Use: 6.0, 5.0 and 4.5 booked hours a day by bay type at maturity. Golfzon's venue guidance is 10 hours a day.
  • Virgin Active: 136 Southern African clubs, 623,000 members (published, March 2026), so 4,580 members a club. 112 clubs take bays.
  • RMB: prime 10.5% (August 2026) plus 1.5%; 12 months interest-only then 60 amortising, per phase; 90% of landed cost financed.
  • Costs: Golfzon software, courses and service at 4% of hardware a year; consumables R24k a bay; card fees 2.5%; marketing 5% of revenue; asset insurance 1% of capex; people R12m a year at maturity (a golf host in each Collection club, four regional technicians, a small central team) and R4m central overhead.
  • Insured shot: R50 for R25,000, one in four players in a bay-hour takes one, 24% ceded to Santam, per the Get Lucky model. Small in rand terms, the whole story in brand terms.
  • FX 18.5, as in the investor model. All figures ex VAT. Depreciation over seven years and tax at 27% are not shown; EBITDA and debt service are what the two readers look at first.

Decisions before this goes on a page

  1. Virgin Active's share. 20% of bay revenue is the base. Alternatives: 15% with a minimum guarantee per club, or 25% with Virgin Active building the room shell. The share is the single lever Virgin Active will negotiate.
  2. Who builds the room. The model assumes Virgin Active supplies shell and power inside its refurbishments. If Virgin Golf builds, capex rises by about R30m and the RMB facility with it.
  3. Equity. R10m base, R26m low. Sources: the Get Lucky round, a Virgin Golf co-investor, Golfzon supplier credit on phase one, or a naming partner paying up front. This is the question RMB will ask first.
  4. The membership add-on. R299 a month with one included hour. Virgin Active may want it inside its Collection tier instead. Either way it is billed by Virgin Active.
  5. Naming partner. R25k a bay a year is deliberately modest. Santam, RMB itself, or a beverage brand could take the chain naming at a multiple of that.
  6. Term and exit. Ten-year exclusive with a Virgin Active buy-out option at year five or ten. RMB's facility runs six years per phase, so the concession must outlast it.
  7. Group rights. First refusal on the UK, Italy, Australia and Asia for Virgin Golf, with each territory financed on its own numbers.
